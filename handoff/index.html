AI Complex Put Desk

A sizing and structure picker for an options book that pays if the AI trade busts. Two desks: the first-order basket (puts on the complex itself), and the second-order desk (instruments with implied vol under 20 that are tied to an AI bust only through financing, power, rates, and FX). Every structure is priced on a skew-calibrated Black-Scholes surface and ranked by payoff per premium dollar at the drawdown you pick. Every number is editable. Nothing here is a live quote or trade advice.

      How to read this

      • Where the edge is. Spreads beat outright options on payoff-per-dollar in almost every case because you sell back the most expensive part of the skew. The cost is a capped payoff, which the blend scoring penalizes. Outright options win when you think the crash overshoots your target, or when the instrument's vol is so low that the option is already nearly free.
      • First-order vs second-order. The first desk buys puts where the beta is high and the vol is 30 to 70. The second desk buys options where the vol is 5 to 18 and the beta is 0.2 to 0.6. Per dollar of premium the second desk wins on convexity if the transmission works, and loses everything if the bust stays contained to equities. The SMH-puts benchmark line on the chart is the honest comparison.
      • Vol is not high right now. On the snapshot date the 52-week IV percentile for NVDA, SMH, AMD, TSM, ARM, CRWV, and MSFT was in the bottom quartile. AVGO, ORCL, and META were above the median. Low IV percentile argues for outright puts and longer dates over spreads, because you are buying vol cheap.
      • Model, not market. Prices come from a skew-adjusted Black-Scholes surface calibrated to a handful of live quotes. Expect real mids to differ by 10 to 20 percent, more on illiquid names and on far-OTM strikes of low-vol ETFs, where the smile is fatter than any linear skew. Before trading, pull the actual chain.
      • Sizing. Premium is the max loss on every bounded structure here. Plan for it to go to zero. Contracts are floored to whole numbers, so the budget is never exceeded.
