AI Complex Put Desk

A sizing and structure picker for a basket of out-of-the-money puts on the AI trade. It prices every structure off a skew-calibrated Black-Scholes surface, ranks them by payoff per premium dollar at the drawdown you pick, and builds a plan inside your premium budget. Every number is editable. Nothing here is a live quote or trade advice.

The scenario is a move in the "AI complex" factor. Each name moves by its beta times that factor. Ranking is payoff at expiry divided by premium paid.

How to read this

  • Where the edge is. Put spreads beat outright puts on payoff-per-dollar in almost every case because you sell back the most expensive part of the skew. The cost is a capped payoff, which the blend scoring penalizes. Outright puts win only when you think the crash overshoots your target.
  • ETF vs single names. SMH and SOXX carry lower implied vol than the single names but also lower beta to the factor. The leaderboard settles that trade-off numerically for the scenario you picked. A basket of single names also carries idiosyncratic risk both ways: one name can crater on its own, or a name can hold up while the complex falls.
  • Vol is not high right now. On the snapshot date the 52-week IV percentile for NVDA, SMH, AMD, TSM, ARM, CRWV, and MSFT was in the bottom quartile. AVGO, ORCL, and META were above the median. Low IV percentile argues for outright puts and longer dates over spreads, because you are buying vol cheap. Check the column before trusting the "vol is high" instinct.
  • Model, not market. Prices come from a skew-adjusted Black-Scholes surface calibrated to a handful of live quotes. Expect real mids to differ by 10 to 20 percent, more on illiquid names. Before trading, pull the actual chain and paste real premiums into the debit field via the haircut, or compare against the leaderboard's IV column.
  • Sizing. Premium is the max loss on every bounded structure here. Plan for it to go to zero. Contracts are floored to whole numbers, so the budget is never exceeded.
